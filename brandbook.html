Brand Overview
Mission

To develop young people into serious writers and thinkers — by giving them a publication that takes their ideas as seriously as they do, and then puts that work in front of the world.

Vision

A world where young people don't wait to be given a seat at the table. They build the table, set the agenda, and write the record of what happened.

Core Values

Intellectual Honesty

We publish what the writer actually thinks. Difficulty is not a reason to simplify. Uncertainty is not a reason to pretend.

Editorial Rigour

Every piece is edited. Every claim is interrogated. Good writing is not decoration — it is the argument.

Global Perspective

We write from Raipur, Morocco, Nigeria, and everywhere in between. No local issue too small. No global event too big.

Youth as Participants

Young people are not stakeholders in the future. They are participants in the present. We don't write for adults — we write as ourselves.

Craft Over Performance

We are not a credential factory. We build writers who can think and thinkers who can write.

No AI Content

Every word published under TYP was written by a human who thought hard about it. Non-negotiable. Always.

Brand Personality
Serious Inquisitive Defiant Intellectual Grounded Rigorous Precise Craft

Precise

We choose words carefully. We do not describe things as "multifaceted" or "complex." We name the specific thing. This precision is visible in every design decision — typography at exact specifications, spacing tokens followed without deviation, no approximate sizing.

Grounded

We write from where we are, not where we wish we were. Raipur is not a limitation. The global exists through the specific. Personal annotations as visual texture. Real writers' names, not "The TYP Team."

Rigorous

Every claim is sourced. Every argument is stress-tested. We do not publish things we cannot defend. Dense information layouts, no filler imagery, data presented cleanly and without inflation.

Defiant

We exist because youth voices are systematically underestimated in serious discourse. Dark editorial aesthetic rather than pastel/approachable. The brand does not smile at you.

Art Director's Rule

"If someone sees TYP for three seconds, they should think: this looks like a publication produced by unusually serious young people. Not a nonprofit. Not a student club. Not a startup."

Logo Don'ts
✕ Don't rotate

Apex always points up. Never flip, tilt, or rotate the prism mark.

✕ Don't recolour freely

Only approved colour versions: on cream, midnight, butter, or sand.

✕ Don't add effects

No drop shadows, glows, gradients, or textures on the logo itself.

✕ Don't distort

Always scale proportionally. Lock aspect ratio. Never stretch.

✕ Don't use on busy backgrounds

Logo must appear on a solid approved surface only.

✕ Don't change the typeface

The wordmark uses Playfair Display exclusively. No substitution.

Voice & Tone
Brand Voice

Direct, not aggressive

Say what you mean. Don't hedge when you have a point. But don't perform confidence you haven't earned. There's a difference.

Serious, not solemn

Gravity without pretension. We're not performing seriousness — we're actually serious. That's a different thing entirely.

Young, not juvenile

Our age is context, not our credential. We don't lead with "as young people" unless it's genuinely the point.

Writing Style Rules
Sentence rhythm

Short punchy sentences mix with longer analytical ones. Vary it. Never five long sentences in a row. Never five short ones either. The pace should feel like a person thinking, not a template running.

Essay openings

Start with a punch — a fact, a moment, a contradiction. Never start with a definition. Never with "In today's world." Never with "Throughout history." The reader is already there. Meet them.

Structural zoom

Individual → Institution → Nation → Global. Show the human moment first. Then explain the system it sits inside. End with a challenge, not a summary. The last line should make the reader feel something.

Citations

Use thinkers purposefully — Klemperer, Tocqueville, Arendt, Orwell. Not to show off. Because the idea earns them. A citation that could be removed without losing the argument should be removed.

Write Like This / Never Like This
✓ Write like this
"Democracies don't die when people are silenced. They die when people stop having the words to name what is happening to them."
✓ Write like this
"China didn't build a wall. It laid 352 metres of rope across a contested shoal and called it administration."
✓ Write like this
"The problem isn't that AI makes decisions. It's that we built the systems that made that possible and then acted surprised."
✕ Never this
"In today's rapidly changing world, it has become increasingly important for young people to engage with global issues."
✕ Never this
"As the next generation of leaders, it is our responsibility to step up and make our voices heard in this critical moment."
✕ Never this
"This article will explore, analyse, and discuss the multifaceted implications of this complex and nuanced issue."

Brand Overview
Mission

To develop young people into serious writers and thinkers — by giving them a publication that takes their ideas as seriously as they do, and then puts that work in front of the world.

Vision

A world where young people don't wait to be given a seat at the table. They build the table, set the agenda, and write the record of what happened.

Core Values

Intellectual Honesty

We publish what the writer actually thinks. Difficulty is not a reason to simplify. Uncertainty is not a reason to pretend.

Editorial Rigour

Every piece is edited. Every claim is interrogated. Good writing is not decoration — it is the argument.

Global Perspective

We write from Raipur, Morocco, Nigeria, and everywhere in between. No local issue too small. No global event too big.

Youth as Participants

Young people are not stakeholders in the future. They are participants in the present. We don't write for adults — we write as ourselves.

Craft Over Performance

We are not a credential factory. We build writers who can think and thinkers who can write.

No AI Content

Every word published under TYP was written by a human who thought hard about it. Non-negotiable. Always.

Brand Personality
Serious Inquisitive Defiant Intellectual Grounded Rigorous Precise Craft

Precise

We choose words carefully. We do not describe things as "multifaceted" or "complex." We name the specific thing. This precision is visible in every design decision — typography at exact specifications, spacing tokens followed without deviation, no approximate sizing.

Grounded

We write from where we are, not where we wish we were. Raipur is not a limitation. The global exists through the specific. Personal annotations as visual texture. Real writers' names, not "The TYP Team."

Rigorous

Every claim is sourced. Every argument is stress-tested. We do not publish things we cannot defend. Dense information layouts, no filler imagery, data presented cleanly and without inflation.

Defiant

We exist because youth voices are systematically underestimated in serious discourse. Dark editorial aesthetic rather than pastel/approachable. The brand does not smile at you.

Art Director's Rule

"If someone sees TYP for three seconds, they should think: this looks like a publication produced by unusually serious young people. Not a nonprofit. Not a student club. Not a startup."

Voice & Tone
Brand Voice

Direct, not aggressive

Say what you mean. Don't hedge when you have a point. But don't perform confidence you haven't earned. There's a difference.

Serious, not solemn

Gravity without pretension. We're not performing seriousness — we're actually serious. That's a different thing entirely.

Young, not juvenile

Our age is context, not our credential. We don't lead with "as young people" unless it's genuinely the point.

Writing Style Rules
Sentence rhythm

Short punchy sentences mix with longer analytical ones. Vary it. Never five long sentences in a row. Never five short ones either. The pace should feel like a person thinking, not a template running.

Essay openings

Start with a punch — a fact, a moment, a contradiction. Never start with a definition. Never with "In today's world." Never with "Throughout history." The reader is already there. Meet them.

Structural zoom

Individual → Institution → Nation → Global. Show the human moment first. Then explain the system it sits inside. End with a challenge, not a summary. The last line should make the reader feel something.

Citations

Use thinkers purposefully — Klemperer, Tocqueville, Arendt, Orwell. Not to show off. Because the idea earns them. A citation that could be removed without losing the argument should be removed.

Write Like This / Never Like This
✓ Write like this
"Democracies don't die when people are silenced. They die when people stop having the words to name what is happening to them."
✓ Write like this
"China didn't build a wall. It laid 352 metres of rope across a contested shoal and called it administration."
✓ Write like this
"The problem isn't that AI makes decisions. It's that we built the systems that made that possible and then acted surprised."
✕ Never this
"In today's rapidly changing world, it has become increasingly important for young people to engage with global issues."
✕ Never this
"As the next generation of leaders, it is our responsibility to step up and make our voices heard in this critical moment."
✕ Never this
"This article will explore, analyse, and discuss the multifaceted implications of this complex and nuanced issue."
